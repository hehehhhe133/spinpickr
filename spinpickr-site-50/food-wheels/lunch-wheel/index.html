Lunch Ideas Wheel

Can't pick lunch? Spin the wheel and go.

One option per line. Min 2, max 15 options.

About this wheel

Lunch is usually a rushed decision, which is why people end up eating the same thing every day. This wheel gives you a quick nudge towards variety without any planning.

Office groups use it to choose a restaurant: list the five places everyone is happy with and spin once. If the group disagrees, the wheel becomes a neutral referee nobody can argue with.

Ways to use it

  • Pick a lunch for the workday or a school tiffin.
  • Choose a restaurant for a team outing from a shortlist.
  • Plan a week of packed lunches with one spin per day.
  • Add leftovers as a slice to avoid food waste.

Making the most of it

Lunch decisions look small but add up: five days a week, fifty weeks a year. A wheel helps you move away from the habit of repeating the same meal. Try keeping two lists, one for packed lunches that you can prepare quickly, and one for eating out with prices attached. For offices, build a shortlist of places within walking distance and let the wheel choose, which avoids the daily negotiation. If you bring lunch from home, spin on Sunday for the whole week and shop for those meals once, which saves both time and money.

Tips

  • Keep the list to what you can realistically get or make on a weekday.
  • Add budget options so the result fits your spending.
  • For groups, let everyone suggest one option before the spin.

How to use it

  • Edit the options in the box under the wheel, one per line.
  • Press the centre button to spin. Use the speaker icon in the corner of the wheel to mute the sound.
  • Spin again, or change the list first. Nothing is stored, so refreshing the page resets everything.

Frequently asked questions

How many options can I add?

Up to 15. More than that makes the labels too small to read.

Does it save my list?

No. Nothing is stored, so refreshing the page resets the wheel.

Can a group use it?

Yes. Agree beforehand that the result is final.
