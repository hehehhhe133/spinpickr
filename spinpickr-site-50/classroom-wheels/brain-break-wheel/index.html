Brain Break Wheel

Quick movement and calm-down breaks, chosen by a spin.

One option per line. Min 2, max 15 options.

About this wheel

Attention drops faster than most lesson plans allow for, and a short reset often does more good than pushing through another ten minutes. The Brain Break Wheel leaves the choice of reset to chance, so you spend your time teaching instead of deciding. All eight starter activities need no equipment and no preparation, which makes them practical in almost any classroom.

Because the result is a surprise, students tend to treat the break as a small event rather than an interruption. The spin itself becomes a signal that work has paused and that everyone is about to move, breathe or laugh together before getting back to it.

Ways to use it

  • Spin after a long listening task, before a test, or when the room turns restless after lunch.
  • Mix calming breaks (deep breaths, silent stand-up) with energetic ones (shake it out, desk push-ups) so you can match the mood of the class.
  • Let a student helper spin and read the result aloud, which also gives them a classroom job.
  • Swap in subject-linked breaks, such as 'act out a verb' during an English lesson or 'count in twos while you stretch' in maths.

Tips

  • Keep breaks to one to three minutes so the class returns to work quickly.
  • Remove any activity that does not suit your space, for example balancing exercises in a crowded room.
  • For younger children, use shorter wording like 'Wiggle fingers' or 'Touch toes', and add a seated option for students who cannot stand easily.

How to use it

  • Edit the options in the box under the wheel, one per line.
  • Press the centre button to spin. Use the speaker icon in the corner of the wheel to mute the sound.
  • Spin again, or change the list first. Nothing is stored, so refreshing the page resets everything.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I use a brain break?

Many teachers use one every 20 to 30 minutes with younger pupils and every 40 to 50 minutes with older ones, but the right rhythm depends on your class and how the lesson is going.

Is it suitable for students with different mobility needs?

Yes. Edit the list so every option can be done seated or in a small space, and add alternatives such as 'Seated stretch' or 'Hand massage'.

Can I use it for online lessons?

Yes. Share your screen and spin; students can follow the activity at their own desks and turn their cameras back on when it ends.
