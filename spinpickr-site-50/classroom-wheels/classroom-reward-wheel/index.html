Classroom Reward Wheel

Make rewards exciting with a spin.

One option per line. Min 2, max 15 options.

About this wheel

Fixed rewards lose their charm quickly, but a prize that might land on anything keeps students interested for much longer. The Classroom Reward Wheel turns routine praise into a moment of anticipation: a student earns a spin, the wheel turns, and the whole class watches to see what comes up.

The default list leans towards free or very cheap rewards, which makes the system easy to keep going all term without a budget. You can edit the prizes at any time to suit your class, your school's rules and the age group you teach.

Ways to use it

  • Give a spin for completed homework, kind behaviour or a week of steady effort.
  • Use it for whole-class goals: when the class reaches its target, one spin decides the group treat.
  • Give rarer prizes a single slice and common ones several by typing them on more than one line.
  • Keep a 'Try again' slice for a bit of fun without giving too much away.

Tips

  • Make sure every prize is something you are genuinely happy to deliver, because students will remember what the wheel promised.
  • Share the rules for earning a spin beforehand so the wheel feels fair and not like a favour.
  • Refresh the prizes every half term so the novelty lasts.

How to use it

  • Edit the options in the box under the wheel, one per line.
  • Press the centre button to spin. Use the speaker icon in the corner of the wheel to mute the sound.
  • Spin again, or change the list first. Nothing is stored, so refreshing the page resets everything.

Frequently asked questions

Do the prizes have to cost money?

No. Privileges like choosing a seat, being line leader or picking the class music cost nothing and are often the most popular.

Can I weight the odds so big prizes are rare?

Yes. Type common prizes on several lines and big ones only once, and the wheel will give the common prizes proportionally more slices.

Is it only for primary classes?

No. Replace the prizes with options suited to older students, such as a deadline extension, choosing a seat for a week or listening to music while working.
