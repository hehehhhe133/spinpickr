Would You Rather Wheel

Spin for a would-you-rather question.

One option per line. Min 2, max 15 options.

About this wheel

A good would-you-rather question starts a conversation in seconds. Every student can answer, there is no wrong choice, and the best part is usually the reasoning that follows.

The questions on this wheel are mild and classroom-safe, from flying versus becoming invisible to no homework versus no tests. Add your own to match your lesson or the interests of your class.

Ways to use it

  • Use it as a warm-up at the start of the lesson to get everyone speaking.
  • Ask students to stand on one side of the room depending on their answer.
  • Turn it into a writing task: students explain their choice in three sentences.
  • Practise persuasive language by asking them to convince a classmate to switch sides.

Making the most of it

Would-you-rather questions work in almost any subject if you bend them slightly. In history, ask 'Would you rather live in ancient Egypt or ancient Rome, and why?'; in science, 'Would you rather explore space or the deep ocean?'. Students practise giving reasons, using comparisons and listening to others, which are skills that matter well beyond the game. For quieter groups, let students discuss with a partner first, and then share their ideas. Write new questions on the board as the year goes on, and invite students to submit their own for the wheel, so they feel ownership of the game.

Tips

  • Ask students to give one reason, which makes the activity more than a simple vote.
  • Preview any questions you add so they stay appropriate for the age group.
  • Spin twice and combine the questions for a funny bonus round.

How to use it

  • Edit the options in the box under the wheel, one per line.
  • Press the centre button to spin. Use the speaker icon in the corner of the wheel to mute the sound.
  • Spin again, or change the list first. Nothing is stored, so refreshing the page resets everything.

Frequently asked questions

Is it classroom appropriate?

Yes. The default questions are mild and kid-friendly.

Can I add questions?

Yes, up to 15.

Does it work for language learners?

Yes. It encourages short, structured answers using the pattern 'I would rather... because...'.
