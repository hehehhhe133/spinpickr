Writing Prompt Wheel

Spin for a story starter.

One option per line. Min 2, max 15 options.

About this wheel

Many writers stall at the start, not because they lack ideas but because they have too many choices. A random prompt removes the decision and gives students something concrete to build on.

The default prompts are short, familiar and flexible, so they work for stories, diary entries, poems and descriptive writing alike.

Ways to use it

  • Use the result as the opening line or the setting of a short story.
  • Spin twice and combine the prompts, for example 'a storm' and 'a talking animal'.
  • Set a five-minute timer for a quick-write that builds writing stamina.
  • Add genre options such as mystery or fantasy on a second wheel.

Making the most of it

Prompts help most when students know what to do with them. After the spin, give a clear task and a time limit: for example, write for ten minutes, include a beginning, middle and end, and use at least three descriptive words. Younger writers can draw first and then write a sentence for each picture. For older students, combine two spins to create a problem that needs solving, then ask them to write the scene from an unusual point of view. Keep a class notebook of the best prompts and the most interesting results, and invite students to propose new prompts for the wheel.

Tips

  • Tell students they can interpret the prompt freely, which supports creativity.
  • Keep a list of favourite prompts and add them to the wheel over time.
  • Share a few finished pieces to show different approaches to the same prompt.

How to use it

  • Edit the options in the box under the wheel, one per line.
  • Press the centre button to spin. Use the speaker icon in the corner of the wheel to mute the sound.
  • Spin again, or change the list first. Nothing is stored, so refreshing the page resets everything.

Frequently asked questions

Is it good for ESL students?

Yes. Short prompts are easy to read and understand.

Can I add genres?

Yes. Replace the prompts or add genre names, up to 15.

Can adults use it?

Yes. It is popular with writing groups and anyone practising creative writing.
