Math Question Picker Wheel

Choose a question number or topic at random.

One option per line. Min 2, max 15 options.

About this wheel

Practice feels different when the next question is chosen by a spinning wheel. Students stay alert because they do not know what is coming, and teachers get an easy way to keep revision varied without planning every step.

The default list covers common topics from addition to geometry, but the wheel is just as useful with question numbers, formulae or the names of students who will solve the next problem at the board.

Ways to use it

  • Replace the list with the numbers 1 to 15 to choose which homework question to review.
  • Pick the topic for a quick starter activity at the beginning of the lesson.
  • Choose which student comes to the board without relying on the same few volunteers.
  • Use two wheels in turn: one for the topic and one for a difficulty level.

Tips

  • Write the chosen question on the board before students begin so everyone starts together.
  • Remove a topic after it has come up if you want full coverage before a test.
  • Reward correct answers with a small privilege, such as choosing the next spin.

How to use it

  • Edit the options in the box under the wheel, one per line.
  • Press the centre button to spin. Use the speaker icon in the corner of the wheel to mute the sound.
  • Spin again, or change the list first. Nothing is stored, so refreshing the page resets everything.

Frequently asked questions

Can it generate math problems?

No. It only picks from the list you provide, so you stay in control of the questions.

Is it good for revision?

Yes. Random topic selection stops revision becoming predictable and can cover weak areas by chance.

Can I use it for other subjects?

Yes. Replace the topics with vocabulary words, historical periods or science units.
