Study Break Activity Wheel

Pick a short, useful break between study sessions.

One option per line. Min 2, max 15 options.

About this wheel

A good break refreshes you, while a bad one pulls you into endless scrolling. The wheel offers healthy options to choose from.

Spin when you finish a study block and use the result for five to ten minutes. The starter list keeps phones out of the picture on purpose.

Ways to use it

  • Take a break after each 25-minute study block.
  • Use it during exam revision to stay fresh.
  • Share it with a study group so everyone breaks together.
  • Add personal favourites such as 'Call a friend'.

Making the most of it

The most useful breaks are short, physical and screen-free. Walking to another room, drinking water or stretching your shoulders gives your eyes and mind a real rest, while scrolling social media usually leaves you more tired than before. Try the wheel with a simple routine: study for 25 to 50 minutes, spin, take the break, then write down the next task before you sit again so you restart quickly. If you study with friends, take the break together and use the wheel to settle who chooses. Over a long revision week, these small pauses add up to better concentration.

Tips

  • Set a timer so a break does not stretch longer than planned.
  • Keep the phone out of the list.
  • Avoid food-only breaks; mix movement and rest.

How to use it

  • Edit the options in the box under the wheel, one per line.
  • Press the centre button to spin. Use the speaker icon in the corner of the wheel to mute the sound.
  • Spin again, or change the list first. Nothing is stored, so refreshing the page resets everything.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a break be?

Five to ten minutes after every 25 to 50 minutes of study works for many students.

Can parents use it?

Yes. It works for any age.

Is it only for students?

No. Anyone working at a desk can use it.
