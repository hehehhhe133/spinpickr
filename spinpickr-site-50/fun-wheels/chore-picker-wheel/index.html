Chore Picker Wheel

Assign household chores without arguments.

One option per line. Min 2, max 15 options.

About this wheel

Chores cause friction when people suspect the list is unfair. A random draw removes that suspicion, because the outcome belongs to the wheel and not to whoever wrote the rota.

The wheel starts with eight common tasks, from dishes to groceries. Edit it to match your home, then spin once per person each week and note the results.

Ways to use it

  • Assign weekly chores in a family meeting.
  • Divide the work in a shared flat so rotation is automatic.
  • Let children spin for their tasks to make chores feel like a game.
  • Use it for a cleaning blitz: spin to see who tackles which room.

Making the most of it

A fair chore system is mostly about visibility. When everyone sees the same random draw, arguments about who always does what lose their force. To keep it going, set a regular spin time, such as Sunday evening, and put the results where everyone can see them. Give each chore a rough time estimate so the work is shared evenly, and consider swapping the hardest ones in rotation. For children, tie the chores to age-appropriate tasks, and celebrate when the list is done with something small, like choosing the family film. Review the wheel every few weeks and update the tasks.

Tips

  • Remove tasks as they are assigned so everyone gets a different job.
  • Write the results on a shared list, a fridge note or a group chat.
  • Pair a hard chore with a small reward to keep spirits up.

How to use it

  • Edit the options in the box under the wheel, one per line.
  • Press the centre button to spin. Use the speaker icon in the corner of the wheel to mute the sound.
  • Spin again, or change the list first. Nothing is stored, so refreshing the page resets everything.

Frequently asked questions

Can kids use it?

Yes, and many children enjoy the spin.

How often should we re-spin?

Weekly is common, but pick a rhythm that suits your household.

What if someone cannot do a chore?

Agree on a swap rule beforehand, such as trading with someone else.
