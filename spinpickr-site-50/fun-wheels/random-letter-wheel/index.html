Random Letter Wheel

Spin for a random letter.

One option per line. Min 2, max 15 options.

About this wheel

Many word games start with a letter chosen at random: Name Place Animal Thing, Scattergories, vocabulary rounds and classroom spelling. Picking a letter from the air is easy, but picking one fairly in front of a group is more fun with a wheel.

The wheel starts with the letters A to M. A wheel can hold up to 15 options, so replace the list with N to Z for the second half of the alphabet, or use consonants only, vowels only or the letters your class is currently studying.

Ways to use it

  • Play Name Place Animal Thing, where everyone writes an answer for each category that starts with the letter.
  • Choose a starting letter for a story or poem in class.
  • Pick a letter for a vocabulary challenge, such as 'name five fruits that start with this letter'.
  • Use it for alphabet games on a long journey.

Making the most of it

Letter games build vocabulary, spelling and quick thinking without feeling like study. In a classroom, you can use the wheel to start a five-minute warm-up: spin, then ask students to write as many words as they can that begin with the letter. For language learners, add a category, such as foods or verbs, to keep the task focused. Families can play at the dinner table with only a pen and paper, and each round takes just a few minutes. To make the game fairer for mixed ages, give younger players an easier category and older players a harder one, such as names of rivers or historical figures.

Tips

  • For the whole alphabet, spin the A to M wheel and the N to Z wheel in turn, or first spin to choose which half to use.
  • Remove difficult letters like Q, X and Z for younger children.
  • Set a time limit, such as one minute, to keep games lively.

How to use it

  • Edit the options in the box under the wheel, one per line.
  • Press the centre button to spin. Use the speaker icon in the corner of the wheel to mute the sound.
  • Spin again, or change the list first. Nothing is stored, so refreshing the page resets everything.

Frequently asked questions

Can it cover the whole alphabet?

One wheel holds up to 15 options, so use two wheels: A to M and N to Z.

Can I use it for spelling practice?

Yes. Ask students to think of or spell words that begin with the letter.

Is it suitable for children?

Yes. Remove the harder letters and use simple categories.
